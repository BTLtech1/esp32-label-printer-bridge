Set up your BTL Print Bridge

Turn your USB label printer into a Wi-Fi printer. Takes about 5 minutes.

Which port is which?

The board has two USB-C ports. It matters which one you use.

UART/COM is for power and setup, USB is for the printer. Once the bridge is running, the USB port only talks to the printer and won't show up on your computer, so always install and update through UART/COM.

Step 1: Plug in the board

Use a USB-C cable to plug the board into this computer. Use the port on the board marked UART (or COM).

Board doesn't light up later on a USB-C phone charger? Many boards need a USB-A to USB-C cable on this port. Your computer works either way.

Step 2: Click Install

A list pops up. Pick the board and click Connect, then follow the prompts.

Which one is the board?

It's usually called USB Single Serial, CH340, CH343, CP210x or USB JTAG/serial. Ignore anything that says Bluetooth.

Still not sure? Click Cancel, unplug the board, and click Install again. The one that disappeared is your board.

Picked the wrong one? No harm done. The installer checks that it found the right kind of board before it changes anything.

This page only works in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open this page over https to install.

Asked to "Erase device"? First install: tick it. Updating a bridge you already set up: leave it unticked to keep your settings.

Nothing shows up in the list? Try a different USB cable: many cables only charge and can't send data. Stuck on connecting? Hold the board's BOOT button while you click Install.

Step 3: Type your Wi-Fi

When it finishes, it asks for your Wi-Fi name and password. Type them in and click Connect. Then click Visit device to open the bridge's page.

Use your 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Most home routers have it; if you see two names, pick the one without "5G".

Step 4: Plug in your printer

Plug your label printer into the board's other port, marked USB, and turn the printer on. The bridge's page will say Printer: connected.

Page says "not connected"?

Check the printer is on and plugged into USB, not UART. Many printers also wait for 5 V on the cable, which these boards don't send by default. The fix is a dab of solder (or conductive pen) across the two tiny pads marked USB-OTG on the board, or a small USB-C OTG adapter with a charger plugged into it. USB hubs and docks don't work. More help.

Step 5: Connect your computer

Open the bridge's page: click Visit device at the end of Step 3, or type the bridge's address into your browser (it's shown in the Wi-Fi step, e.g. http://192.168.1.50).

On that page, follow the Add to a Windows PC box:

  1. Right-click the Start button → Terminal (Admin) → Yes.
  2. Click Copy on the bridge's page, paste into the terminal (Ctrl+V), press Enter.
  3. Press Enter to use the driver it finds, pick your label size, click OK. A test page prints.

Done. Press Ctrl+P in any app and choose Label Printer (Wi-Fi).

Your printer's driver must be installed on the PC first (plug the printer in by USB once, or use the maker's installer). Mac: see the guide.

Wi-Fi step didn't show up?

Join the Wi-Fi network BTL-Printer-Setup from your phone or computer. A page opens (or go to http://192.168.4.1). Type your Wi-Fi there and save.
