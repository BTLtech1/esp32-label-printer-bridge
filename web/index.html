Set up your BTL Print Bridge

Turn your USB label printer into a Wi-Fi printer. Takes about 5 minutes.

Which port is which?

The board has two USB-C ports. It matters which one you use.

UART/COM is for power and setup, USB is for the printer. Once the bridge is running, the USB port only talks to the printer and won't show up on your computer, so always install and update through UART/COM.

  1. Plug in the board

    Use a USB-C cable to plug the board into this computer. Use the port on the board marked UART (or COM).

    Board doesn't light up later on a USB-C phone charger? Many boards need a USB-A to USB-C cable on this port. Your computer works either way.

  2. Click Install

    A list pops up. Pick the board and click Connect, then follow the prompts.

    Which one is the board?

    It's usually called USB Single Serial, CH340, CH343, CP210x or USB JTAG/serial. Ignore anything that says Bluetooth.

    Still not sure? Click Cancel, unplug the board, and click Install again. The one that disappeared is your board.

    Picked the wrong one? No harm done. The installer checks that it found the right kind of board before it changes anything.

    This page only works in Chrome or Edge on a computer. Open this page over https to install.

    Asked to "Erase device"? First install: tick it. Updating a bridge you already set up: leave it unticked to keep your settings.

    Nothing shows up in the list? Try a different USB cable: many cables only charge and can't send data. Stuck on connecting? Hold the board's BOOT button while you click Install.

  3. Type your Wi-Fi

    When it finishes, it asks for your Wi-Fi name and password. Type them in and click Connect. When it says it's connected, click Continue, close the box, and go on to Step 4.

    Use your 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi. Most home routers have it; if you see two names, pick the one without "5G".

    Wi-Fi step didn't show up?

    Join the Wi-Fi network BTL-Printer-Setup from your phone or computer. A page opens (or go to http://192.168.4.1). Type your Wi-Fi there and save.

  4. Plug in your printer

    Plug your label printer into the board's other port, marked USB, and turn the printer on. The light on the board turns green when the printer is ready. (Red = no printer yet, purple = not on your Wi-Fi yet.)

    Light stays red?

    Check the printer is on and plugged into USB, not UART. Many printers also wait for 5 V on the cable, which these boards don't send by default. The fix is a dab of solder (or conductive pen) across the two tiny pads marked USB-OTG on the board, or a small USB-C OTG adapter with a charger plugged into it. USB hubs and docks don't work. More help.

  5. Add it to your Windows PC

    1. Right-click the Start button, then Terminal (Admin), then click Yes.
    2. Copy this line, paste it into that window (Ctrl+V), and press Enter:
      irm https://btltech1.github.io/esp32-label-printer-bridge/windows.txt | iex
    3. It finds your bridge by itself (with more than one, press Enter to pick the new one). Press Enter to use the driver it suggests, press Enter again to name it after your printer (or type your own name), pick your label size, click OK. A test page prints.

    Done. Press Ctrl+P in any app and choose your printer's name with (Wi-Fi) after it.

    At the end it opens the bridge's page, and offers a desktop shortcut to it (type Y if you want one). That page is where you save fliers (Step 6), see the status and update the bridge.

    Your printer's driver must be installed on this PC first (plug the printer in by USB once, or use the maker's installer). Got more than one bridge? Run the line once for each. Mac: see the guide.

  6. Saved fliers optional

    Save up to 5 fliers on the bridge, then print them with the board's BOOT button. No computer needed.

    Type it once and this page remembers it. The name is btl-printer- plus 4 letters/numbers; you can type just those 4. Don't know it? Run the line from Step 5 again: it finds your bridge, shows its name and opens its page. It's also in your router's device list.

    1. Open the bridge's page: use the box above (or the BTL Print Bridge desktop shortcut, if you made one in Step 5). Under Saved fliers, pick a flier, open Change this flier and click Save next print here. The light turns yellow while it waits.
    2. Within 5 minutes, print your flier from your computer as usual. The bridge saves it instead of printing it.
    3. Print it with the BOOT button: click once for Flier 1, twice quickly for Flier 2, and so on up to 5. For a batch, hold the last click for 2 seconds (Flier 2: click, click-and-hold). Press again to stop a batch. A batch is 10 copies; change it next to each flier on the bridge's page.

    Optional: add your own buttons

    Want one button per flier? Wire each button to a pin, GPIO 4, 5, 6, 7, 15 for Flier 1 to 5, and its other wire to GND. No resistors needed; female jumper wires push right onto the board's pins. Press = 1 copy, hold 2 seconds = batch, press again to stop.

    A flier is saved exactly as your printer's driver made it, so print it on the same kind of printer.
